# Crossroads: A Student's Chronicle

---

## I. Opening (invocation + authority + purpose)

**Invocation**: Sustenance — the sustainer of mind and body, without which no labor is possible.

**Authority acknowledged**: UC Berkeley — "this great institution of learning" (or similar). Mention its scale, its resources, its claims.

**Identity stated**: A first-year student, newly arrived from across an ocean, a seeker of applied knowledge.

**Purpose stated**: Because the dining hall is everywhere displayed (Instagram, posters, promos) but nowhere *recorded* — images that cannot be searched, information that passes through the eye and is lost — this text is written so that what it means to eat here might be preserved for those who come after.

---

## II. Social Position (who I am)

- First-year student at UC Berkeley, applied mathematics
- From China — this is my first time in the United States, first time traveling abroad after COVID
- Live at Foothill, on the northeastern edge of campus
- Housed in a double room with a Chinese roommate assigned at random — two strangers from the same country, paired by lottery
- I eat at Crossroads for lunch and dinner most days: it has the most choices of any hall

+ Use this section to establish the "relational identity" the assignment asks for — I am a student, a newcomer, a Chinese person in America, a resident of Foothill, one of thousands housed by lottery.

---

## III. The Building (Crossroads described)

- The largest dining hall on campus; two blocks south of the campus's southern border
- White-ish building with tall ceilings, long steel bars spanning the roof
- Entrance wall is entirely glass — good light there, but the inner walls are enclosed, windowless
- Square O-shape with an open patio in the middle
- Belongs to Berkeley Student Services — a broader institution for serving students
- Hosts "Cramming at Crossroads" during RRR week each semester

**The Two Doors**:
- One with a grey steel handle, pulled open by hand
- One with a wheelchair button on the wall — used constantly by people who are simply lazy
- The wheelchair door is slower; pulling the handle is faster, but many choose to wait anyway

---

## IV. Power (how the dining hall operates)

**The Card Reader**:
- At the entrance, a lane with steel bar handles like the queues of Disneyland
- A swipe of the student ID card produces a faint beep and a light that changes from red to yellowish-green — this is the ritual of entry
- Behind the counter, a staff member sits on the standard black dining hall chair, rotates occasionally, never stands
- Debit and credit cards are permitted at a slight surcharge

**The Leveling**:
- Undergraduates, graduate student instructors, tutors — all eat from the same buffet
- All classes, all races, all eat here
- Sometimes people slip through the exit when the lane is crowded — the staff cannot watch everyone

**Access and intrusion**:
- Homeless people enter when someone buys them a meal
- School groups crowd the lane — visiting high schoolers, middle schoolers, small children, their voices loud, their excitement uncontained
- Parents paying by credit card require the staff to change settings on the terminal, slowing everything

+ Power operates through the card reader (gate), the staff (guardians), the rules (one plate per swipe, no carry-out of unhandled items), but also through the strange equality of the space — authority is present but distributed.

## V. Culture and Everyday Life (the clockwise tour)

**The Journey Down**:
- 12-15 minutes on foot from Foothill: down the hill past the business school, through the law school, cutting across Unit 1
- The bus takes 20 minutes and does not come often — walking is faster

**I. The Allergy Counter** (my first stop)
- In the corner nearest the bathroom — not ideal
- Covered pots, unreadable at first: what is "masala"? A meaningless sound
- I learned: mushrooms, bok choy, broccoli, sometimes sprouts
- I go here first because they have their own plates — for the protection of allergic students, their plates are kept separate, which saves me from carrying multiple trays

**II. The Pasta Station** (next to it, farther from the bathroom)
- Carrots cut into small rounds — watery, a plastic texture, like tofu pressed to high strength
- Usually no line

**III. The Frying Stand**
- Hot dogs, burgers, sandwiches, fries, chips
- In the morning: pancakes with maple syrup so sweet it might as well be candy
- I take only chips and fries if they have them

**IV. The Main Dish Stand**
- The one that changes: African, Chinese, Vietnamese, Black History Week food
- I take rice sometimes, green beans, maybe spring rolls, occasionally meat

**V. The Sandwich Counter**
- I go only for the salmon salad — chopped fine, neutral, I never tire of it

**VI. The Soup Station**
- Dark-colored, thick, often terrible — some soups feel more like congealed substances than liquid
- The bean tuscan is acceptable: mushrooms, dark leaves of unknown species, rice floating
- The cheese chowder is too heavy

**VII. The Drinks Station**
- Sodas in abundance, orange juice, milk in a machine so tall it stands nearly at my height
- Lemonade the color of brushed gold, with a faint mint-like smell — and sweet enough to be classed among the syrups of the apothecary

**VIII. The Lemongrass Stand**
- Rows of small sauce bottles, palm-high, so many that I doubt anyone knows them all
- Always has chopsticks — I take them with my left hand as a form of practice, leaving my right hand for my phone
- Spring rolls, green dumplings, cha shiu bao with a pink filling the color of something I would not trust

**IX. Chocolate Milk Stand**
- Runs out quickly
- A white towel folded beneath the machine to catch the perpetual leak

**X. The Pizza Section**
- Cheese pizza about four days out of seven
- Once I saw the netting above the pizza fall onto it; I told the staff and they put it back — this told me something about what standards mean here

**The Meal**:
- I sit at the steel tables — all chairs black, all the same size
- On the table: a paper box of tissues, a white cup of salt, a black cup of pepper
- I eat for 20 to 30 minutes, sometimes going back for more
- I watch people: couples, homeless men who clean up after themselves and those who do not, classmates I half-recognize
- One such classmate — who drank tea and read books with his legs crossed in a posture I associated with old European wealth — became my roommate for the next year

**The Exit**:
- Trash room: steamy, smelling of the dishwashing station behind it
- I take a banana if there is one, maybe an orange, a cookie if they have butter or chocolate chip
- Sometimes ice cream, depending on the weather
- Then I walk back up the hill

---

## VI. Critique (indirect)

- The institution commands resources beyond the reckoning of most kingdoms; it employs a great host of diligent servants
- Yet the food remains remarkable for its gap between effort and result
- There was a fish, served on a Tuesday, that made me sick for two days. The smell of it now follows me like a warning. I give it a wide berth, as one would a decaying thing by the roadside.

+ No accusations. Just the facts, placed next to each other.

---

## VII. Closing Gratitude

- Food and sleep are the foundation of all things
- The dining hall has run reliably all year
- It has kept me alive
- I am thankful
